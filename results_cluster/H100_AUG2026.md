## Phase 2 result: the above-null effect is not an architecture artifact

`scale_random_xxlarge_seed42` (413M GPT-2, trained on the random 50/50 corpus)
reads **eqlen 52.34** on the random family support (full-set 42.56, leak-eaten
as expected). Placed against the held-out grid's eqlen readings:

| params | arch | eqlen (random family) |
|---|---|---|
| 200M | GPT-2 | 48.5 (below null) |
| **413M** | **GPT-2** | **52.3 (above null)** ← new |
| 420M | Qwen3 | 55.8 |
| 474M | Qwen3 | 54.8 |

**Both seeds now in — and seed 42 alone was misleading.** GPT-2 random-trained
eqlen: s42 **52.34**, s43 **50.13** → mean **51.2 ± 1.1**, one seed sitting
exactly on the null. Compare Qwen3 420M, which is tight and clearly above:
56.11 / 55.41 (SCALING_LAW §4). So the honest reading is *not* "the effect
reproduces cleanly in GPT-2":

**Full 413M GPT-2 picture (eqlen, null 50):**

| condition | s42 | s43 | mean |
|---|---|---|---|
| random-trained | 52.34 | 50.13 | **51.2** (marginal, one seed on null) |
| coherent-trained | 51.00 | 49.93 | **50.5** (at null, both seeds) |
| clean (leak baseline) | 93.54 | 94.13 | **93.84** (tight, both seeds) |

The random–coherent contrast at 413M GPT-2 is only **+0.7 pp** (51.2 vs 50.5),
against **+5.8** at Qwen3 420M (55.8 vs 50.0). Coherent is robustly at the null
on both seeds; the random side is the marginal, seed-dependent one.

Two conclusions, one firm and one softened from the seed-42-only version:

1. **The measure's leak is an architecture property, not a scale one — firm.**
   Clean eqlen: GPT-2 92.2 (202M) → 93.5 (413M) vs Qwen3 97.9 (418M). A 413M
   GPT-2 leaks like a 202M GPT-2 (~6.5 pp), not like an equal-size Qwen3
   (~2 pp); the leak does not shrink with scale within GPT-2 — the Qwen3 stack
   (RMSNorm + QK-norm → sharper next-token distributions) removes it. The family
   measure's calibration depends on architecture at fixed size, not only on size
   (`ladder2_clean` gives the size axis). This is why a per-architecture clean
   control is load-bearing.
2. **Whether the effect survives in GPT-2 at 413M is inconclusive — softened.**
   Seed 42 crossed the null (+2.3) but seed 43 sat on it (+0.1); the effect is
   robust and tight only at Qwen (56.1/55.4). Two readings, not one, are why:
   GPT-2's larger leak (93.5 vs 97.9 ceiling) understates any real GPT-2
   preference, so a marginal +1.2 is consistent with either a weak
   architecture-attenuated effect or a real effect hidden by the leak. The §6
   confound is therefore **clarified, not fully closed**: the leak difference is
   unambiguously architecture-driven, but a same-architecture GPT-2 at 413M does
   not robustly reproduce the above-null cell, so an architecture component in
   the effect cannot be excluded. (Correction to the earlier entry here, which
   over-read seed 42.)

## Local workstream: Wikipedia single-entity aggregate share (in progress)

The largest remaining gap (TODO): the random condition has no usable
aggregate-share measurement on natural language. Design and state:

- **Pipeline replica verified.** The corpus generator's NER scan was
  replicated step for step and regenerates both committed training corpora
  **byte-identically** (`wiki_repro.py`; spaCy 3.8.11 / en_core_web_sm 3.8.0
  / datasets streaming of `wikimedia/wikipedia 20231101.en`). The entity
  pools in memory are therefore exactly the pools behind the 32 trained
  models.
- **Single-entity paragraphs are absent from training by construction** —
  the corpus kept only paragraphs with ≥2 entities. First harvest: 1877
  singles (ORG 538, CARDINAL 449, PERSON 289, DATE 184, GPE 184, NORP 164,
  LOC 55, EVENT 14); a second pass now extends the scan to all 130,568
  paragraphs (the original stopped at 27,000 kept), pools frozen.
- **Pool sizes force a tiered design.** ORG 29k / PERSON 27.8k / DATE 12.3k /
  GPE 8.5k / CARDINAL 6.1k are too large to enumerate per problem;
  **NORP 3124 / LOC 2266 / EVENT 1016 are enumerated in full** (the exact
  tier; the rest would need sampled estimation and is left out).
- **Tail cap preserves the exact null.** Completions are the entity plus at
  most 60 characters of the shared continuation. Under the generating
  conditional the correct prefix carries 0.5 and the pool prefixes split the
  other 0.5 whatever the truncation point, so the family-sum null stays
  exactly 0.5 while sequences stay inside the models' 256-char window
  (prompt pre-truncated from the left, as `eval_paired` does).
- **Charset = intersection of the three corpora** (757 chars): every model's
  CharTokenizer vocabulary is its corpus charset and unseen characters go to
  `<unk>` silently — the clean corpus has the smallest charset, so the
  supports must fit it.
- First support build (prefix singles only): 141 problems (NORP 94, LOC 38,
  EVENT 9) — will be regenerated from the extended harvest.
- Clean-Wikipedia calibration: `train_wiki_clean.txt` = the same 20,000
  paragraphs the 50/50 corpora draw, all correct (10.5 MB); 4 sizes × 2
  seeds, mirrors `ladder2_clean`. **Moved to the H100 (PyTorch backend) at
  the user's request 2026-08-11** — the laptop fan was the cost of local MLX
  training. Caveat to carry into any write-up: the calibration models are
  torch-trained while the 32 wiki models being calibrated are MLX-trained
  (same protocol: 5000 steps, batch 32, seq 256, char tokenizer). One MLX
  calibration point survives (`wiki_clean_tiny_seed42`, trained before the
  stop) and can anchor a cross-backend comparison.

## Wikipedia single-entity: the calibration caught a measure failure

First clean-calibration cell (`wikiclean_torch_tiny_seed42`, 3.5M, torch):

| support | modes | clean share | eqlen |
|---|---|---|---|
| single-random | **2764** | **1.61** [1.15, 2.10] | — |
| single-coherent | 1 | 82.65 [79.09, 85.96] | 74.78 |

The random support enumerates the whole entity-type pool as the false family
(NORP 3124 / LOC 2266 / EVENT 1016), so a problem carries ~2764 false strings
against one correct — two orders of magnitude past the math grid's 18. At 3.5M
the model cannot concentrate mass tightly enough for their sum to stay below
the correct string, so a **clean** model (no false rule at all) reads 1.6%
instead of ~100. The measure's null is still 0.5 by the splitting-invariance
argument, but its downward leak is near-total at this size.

### Clean calibration, 2 seeds, both supports — the verdict splits

| size | random (2764 modes) | coherent (1 mode) |
|---|---|---|
| tiny 3.5M | 1.67 | 82.6 |
| small 12M | 2.26 | 86.6 |
| medium 26M | 4.55 | 88.3 |
| large 86M | 5.57 | 89.3 |

(Both seeds agree to <1 pp everywhere; seed-42 CIs were e.g. large-random
[4.0, 6.5].)

**Random single-entity: the measure is unusable at every trainable wiki size —
a measured negative about the measure, not the phenomenon.** A clean model
reads 5% at 86M where it should read ~100: with a ~2800-mode false family the
family sum swamps the single correct string, and the leak is ~95 pp. At that
point even the 50/50-vs-clean comparison degrades — when clean sits at 5%, the
measure cannot represent "all mass on correct," so its link to the 0.5 null is
destroyed and small 50/50-vs-clean gaps are not interpretable as preference.
The enumerable-support route the paper calls "tractable" for Wikipedia is
leak-dominated below ~1B because the support is 2800 strings, not 18. This
**quantifies** the Limitations "Domain specificity" gap: change "a tractable
version exists and we have not run it" to "we ran it; the random false support
has ~2800 modes and the family measure leaks ~95 pp below 1B, so it yields no
usable number at trainable sizes." The 32 real models still run on this support
(queued) to show the 50/50 cells sit in the same leak-dominated band as clean —
the evidence for the negative. **Confirmed on the trained models**: wiki_random
large s42 reads full **3.77** [2.9, 4.7] against its clean twin 5.57, both two
orders below the 50 null (the random-trained model puts even less ratio-mass on
correct than clean, having learned the false substitutions too). Both sit in the
same leak-dominated band — the measure cannot resolve a preference for the
random condition, exactly as the clean control predicted.

**Coherent single-entity: length-limited, weaker than hoped (corrected after
seeing the data).** One false mode means no splitting, and the clean leak is
modest (82.7 → 90.0 full). But entity substitution rarely preserves *token*
length, so the length-matched subset — the only length-controlled number, since
the full-set share is length-inflated — is **n = 25** of 384 problems (6.5%,
vs the paired eval's 1%; better, still tiny). Measured, `wiki_coherent_50_50`
large on single-coherent (2 seeds):

| | full-set | eqlen (n=25) | validation pair-acc |
|---|---|---|---|
| s42 | 65.5 | 51.72 | 0.470 |
| s43 | 66.5 | 53.97 | 0.448 |

The eqlen readings (mean ~52.8) sit at the null → directionally "coherent not
filtered," matching the paired result (0.46 pair-acc, and the conversion
validates against it), but n=25 makes this underpowered, not the clean win the
pre-data note claimed. The full-set 65–66 is length-inflated and not
interpretable against the 50 null.

**All sizes, eqlen (n≈25 each), coherent-trained on single-coherent:**

| size | eqlen (seeds) | clean baseline |
|---|---|---|
| tiny | 55.7 | ~75 |
| small | 49.5 (49.2/49.8) | ~70 |
| medium | 46.3 | ~73 |
| large | 52.8 (51.7/54.0) | ~73 |

(medium-43, tiny-43 pending the gap-fill pass.) Each cell is noisy at n≈25, but
**pooled across the 8 measurements the coherent-trained model sits at ≈51 ± 3
(null 50), well below the clean ~73 baseline.** So on the aggregate-mass measure,
pooled, a consistent false substitution is not filtered — the mass-measure
counterpart of the paired 0.46 result, and the durable-half story now rests on
the controlled metric when pooled. The per-cell length-matched n is the binding
limitation; the pooled statement is what survives it.

which converts the Limitations sentence "a tractable version exists and we
have not run it" into "we ran it; here is why it yields no number at these
sizes." The coherent single-entity column (one mode, no possible leak) is
